  \begin{abstract}
    Starting from the axiomatic foundation of admissible non-injective
    transitions~\cite{FoundationM}, we derive the structure of gauge theory without
    introducing it as a postulate.
    Gauge charges are identified as invariants of equivalence classes in the projection
    fibre: two configurations are gauge-equivalent when they project to the same
    observable state.
    The group of fibre-preserving transformations constitutes the gauge group, with
    $\mathrm{U}(1)$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ identified as natural
    candidates from the admissibility structure.
    The gauge connection arises as the correction required to compare admissible fibres
    over neighbouring points, and Yang\textendash Mills dynamics follow from minimising
    the curvature of this connection.
    No dynamical substrate is required at any step; the derivations rest solely on
    Axioms~A1--A4 of~\cite{FoundationM} and the spectral results of the O-series.
    Effective spacetime geometry is derived in the companion paper~\cite{Q6b}.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:intro}

    \subsection*{Position in the programme}

      The companion papers~\cite{FoundationM,Q1,Q2} establish the quantum-mechanical
      structure of the Cosmochrony framework: the proto-state, phase coherence, the
      Born rule, the singlet correlator, and the Tsirelson bound, all derived from the
      admissibility structure of non-injective projection.
      These results concern the \emph{internal} structure of the admissible fibre at a
      single transition step.

      The present paper addresses one macroscopic consequence of allowing the projection
      structure to vary from point to point in the effective continuum: the conserved
      invariants of the projection fibre and the symmetry group they generate.
      The answer is gauge charges and gauge groups (§\ref{sec:charges}), with
      Yang\textendash Mills dynamics emerging from fibre transport (§\ref{sec:ym}).
      Effective spacetime geometry is derived in the companion paper~\cite{Q6b}.

    \subsection*{What is and is not derived in this paper}

      This paper derives the \emph{structure} of gauge theory from the admissibility
      framework.
      It does not derive specific coupling constants or the precise matter content of
      the Standard Model.
      These remain open and are identified as directions for future work in
      Section~\ref{sec:open}.

      The derivations are structural: they identify which gauge groups are
      \emph{admissible} given the constraints, not which specific values their
      parameters take.

    \subsection*{Organisation}

      Section~\ref{sec:charges} derives gauge charges as fibre invariants and
      identifies the gauge group.
      Section~\ref{sec:connection} derives the gauge connection from the requirement of
      consistent transport between neighbouring fibres.
      Section~\ref{sec:ym} derives Yang\textendash Mills dynamics from the minimisation
      of connection curvature.
      Section~\ref{sec:open} identifies the open directions.

  \section{Gauge Charges as Fibre Invariants}
    \label{sec:charges}

    \subsection{Charges from non-injectivity}

      The non-injectivity of $\Pi_n$ (Axiom~A2 of~\cite{FoundationM}) means that
      multiple configurations in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ project to the same observable
      state $O_n$.
      These configurations are physically indistinguishable at the observable level
      but may carry distinct internal structure.

      \begin{definition}[Gauge charge]
        \label{def:gauge-charge}
        A \emph{gauge charge} is a functional
        \begin{equation}
          Q : \Omega_n^{(c)} \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{R}
        \end{equation}
        that is constant on projection fibres:
        \begin{equation}
          \Pi_n(\omega_1) = \Pi_n(\omega_2)
          \;\Longrightarrow\;
          Q(\omega_1) = Q(\omega_2).
        \end{equation}
      \end{definition}

      A gauge charge is therefore an observable that cannot be resolved by $\Pi_n$
      itself --- it lives in the kernel structure of $\Pi_n$ --- but is well-defined
      on the fibre.

      \begin{definition}[Gauge group]
        \label{def:gauge-group}
        The \emph{gauge group} $\mathcal{G}$ is the group of transformations
        $\omega \mapsto g \cdot \omega$ that preserve the projection:
        \begin{equation}
          \mathcal{G}
          \;:=\;
          \bigl\{g \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Omega_n^{(c)}) \mid
          \Pi_n(g \cdot \omega) = \Pi_n(\omega) \text{ for all } \omega \bigr\}.
        \end{equation}
      \end{definition}

      $\mathcal{G}$ consists precisely of the fibre-preserving symmetries: it acts
      within $\Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ without affecting the observable output.

      \begin{remark}[Gauge invariance as structural necessity]
        The gauge group is not introduced by hand.
        It is forced by the structure of $\Pi_n$: as soon as $\Pi_n$ is non-injective,
        there exist non-trivial fibre-preserving transformations, and hence a
        non-trivial gauge group.
        Gauge invariance is a structural consequence of Axiom~A2, not an additional
        postulate.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{Identification of the gauge groups}

      Three natural candidates emerge from the fibre structure established in
      Q1~\cite{Q1} and Q2~\cite{Q2}:

      \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{$\mathrm{U}(1)$}: global phase rotations
        $\omega \mapsto e^{i\theta}\omega$ within the complex fibre $\mathbb{C}^q$.
        These preserve $|\omega|$ and hence the observable norm, while acting
        non-trivially on the phase — which is in the kernel of $\Pi_n$ by the phase
        invariance of $\sigma_c(n)$~\cite{O12}.

        \item \emph{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$}: the stable fixed point of the admissibility
        flow established in~\cite{Q2} Theorem~8.1.
        Its fibre-preserving action on $\mathbb{C}^2$ is the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
        structure derived in~\cite{Q1}.

        \item \emph{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$}: a structurally motivated candidate for a
        higher-dimensional internal symmetry of the fibre, corresponding to multiple
        co-admissible internal directions beyond $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
        Its identification from the O-series admissibility data is an open direction
        (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
      \end{itemize}

      \begin{remark}[Status of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$]
        The identification of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a gauge group is a structural
        hypothesis, not a derived result.
        Establishing it would require finding, within the O-series spectral data, a
        co-admissibility pattern for a group with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ structure analogous
        to the $\lambda_{1/2} = \lambda_{3/2}$ co-admissibility of~\cite{Q2}.
        This is identified as a medium-term open direction.
      \end{remark}

  \section{The Gauge Connection}
    \label{sec:connection}

    \subsection{The problem of comparing neighbouring fibres}

      The following development assumes that the admissible fibre $F_n$ admits an
      effective continuum description in which it forms a smooth fibre bundle over an
      effective spacetime manifold.
      The precise sense in which this limit holds is established conditionally
      in~\cite{Q5a}; it is used here as a working hypothesis.

      For each point $x$ in the effective manifold, the fibre $F_x = \Pi^{-1}(x)$
      carries the internal gauge structure of Section~\ref{sec:charges}.

      When the projection $\Pi(x)$ varies from point to point, the fibres $F_x$ and
      $F_{x+dx}$ are not canonically identified.
      A state $\psi(x)$ transforming under $\mathcal{G}$ acquires an additional term
      under differentiation:
      \begin{equation}
        \partial_\mu(g(x)\,\psi(x))
        \;=\; g(x)\,\partial_\mu\psi(x) + (\partial_\mu g(x))\,\psi(x),
      \end{equation}
      which is not covariant under local gauge transformations $g(x) \in \mathcal{G}$.

    \subsection{Gauge connection from fibre transport}

      \begin{definition}[Gauge connection]
        \label{def:connection}
        A \emph{gauge connection} $A_\mu$ is the correction required to define a
        covariant derivative
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:covariant-derivative}
          D_\mu \;:=\; \partial_\mu + A_\mu
        \end{equation}
        such that $D_\mu\psi$ transforms homogeneously under local gauge
        transformations.
      \end{definition}

      \begin{proposition}[Admissibility interpretation of $A_\mu$]
        \label{prop:connection-admissibility}
        In the projection framework, $A_\mu(x)$ encodes the local variation of the
        admissible fibre structure: it is the infinitesimal correction needed to
        transport an admissible configuration from the fibre at $x$ to the fibre
        at $x + dx$ consistently with the admissibility constraints.
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}[Structural argument]
        The admissibility constraints (A1--A3 of~\cite{FoundationM}) define, at each
        point $x$, a fibre $F_x$ of structurally compatible configurations.
        When $\Pi(x)$ varies, the fibres $F_x$ and $F_{x+dx}$ are related by the
        differential of the projection map.
        Consistency of the admissibility condition across neighbouring fibres ---
        i.e.\ that a configuration admissible at $x$ maps to an admissible
        configuration at $x + dx$ --- requires a linear correction of the form
        $A_\mu(x)\,dx^\mu$.
        This correction is precisely the gauge connection.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Relation to the holonomy argument]
        The white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} establishes that non-injective admissible
        projections generically have non-trivial holonomy, which is the minimal
        structural condition for a gauge symmetry to arise as a descriptive invariance.
        Proposition~\ref{prop:connection-admissibility} is the local infinitesimal
        version of this global statement.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Yang\textendash Mills Dynamics}
    \label{sec:ym}

    \subsection{Field strength as curvature of the connection}

      \begin{definition}[Field strength]
        \label{def:field-strength}
        The \emph{field strength} associated with the gauge connection $A_\mu$ is the
        curvature of the covariant derivative:
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:field-strength}
          F_{\mu\nu} \;:=\; [D_\mu, D_\nu]
          \;=\; \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu + [A_\mu, A_\nu].
        \end{equation}
      \end{definition}

      $F_{\mu\nu}$ measures the failure of commutativity of parallel transport around
      an infinitesimal loop --- equivalently, the non-commutativity of the admissibility
      constraints in two neighbouring directions.

    \subsection{Yang\textendash Mills action from admissibility minimisation}

      \begin{proposition}[Yang\textendash Mills action from coherence minimisation]
        \label{prop:ym-action}
        The natural dynamical principle for the gauge connection is minimisation of the
        deformation of the projection structure, leading to the action
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:ym-action}
          S_{\mathrm{YM}}[A]
          \;=\; \int \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}\bigr)\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x.
        \end{equation}
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}[Structural argument]
        By Proposition~\ref{prop:connection-admissibility}, $A_\mu$ encodes the local
        variation of the fibre structure.
        The curvature $F_{\mu\nu}$ measures the incompatibility of this variation around
        closed loops: a flat connection ($F_{\mu\nu} = 0$) corresponds to a globally
        consistent fibre structure, while non-zero curvature signals inconsistency.
        The admissibility principle (A3 of~\cite{FoundationM}) favours configurations
        that preserve the full multiplicity of open directions without premature
        selection.
        Translated to the gauge connection, this principle selects configurations that
        minimise incompatibility, i.e.\ minimise $\|F_{\mu\nu}\|^2$.
        The action~\eqref{eq:ym-action} is the unique gauge-invariant,
        Lorentz-invariant, second-order functional of $A_\mu$ that achieves this.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{corollary}[Yang\textendash Mills equations]
        Variation of~\eqref{eq:ym-action} with respect to $A_\mu$ yields
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:ym-equations}
          D_\mu F^{\mu\nu} \;=\; 0,
        \end{equation}
        the Yang\textendash Mills equations in vacuum.
      \end{corollary}

      \begin{remark}[Status]
        \label{rem:ym-status}
        Proposition~\ref{prop:ym-action} is a structural motivation, not a complete
        proof.
        The uniqueness claim requires showing that~\eqref{eq:ym-action} is the
        \emph{only} admissibility-compatible action at second order in $A_\mu$.
        This follows in the Born\textendash Infeld limit from the results
        of~\cite{BornInfeld} (the BI action reduces to the Yang\textendash Mills action
        in the weak-field regime), but a direct derivation from the axioms
        of~\cite{FoundationM} alone is an open step identified in
        Section~\ref{sec:open}.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Open Directions}
    \label{sec:open}

    \paragraph{Direct derivation of Yang\textendash Mills uniqueness.}
      Proposition~\ref{prop:ym-action} gives a structural motivation for the
      Yang\textendash Mills action.
      Its uniqueness --- that $S_{\mathrm{YM}}$ is the \emph{only}
      admissibility-compatible second-order action --- follows from the
      Born\textendash Infeld limit~\cite{BornInfeld} but not yet from Foundation~M
      axioms alone.

    \paragraph{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ from admissibility.}
      The identification of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a gauge group requires finding a
      co-admissibility pattern for an $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-type group in the O-series
      spectral data.
      The analogue of the $\lambda_{1/2} = \lambda_{3/2}$ result on $2I$ (established
      in~\cite{Q2}) must be found for a group with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ structure.

    \paragraph{Coupling constants.}
      The present paper identifies which gauge groups are admissible.
      It does not determine the numerical values of coupling constants
      ($g_s$, $g_w$, $\alpha$).
      These are expected to emerge from the quantitative spectral data of the O-series
      via the transfer chain $c_\chi \to \delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \to \beta^* \approx 0.126$,
      but the explicit identification is open.
