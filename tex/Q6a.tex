  \begin{abstract}
    Starting from the axiomatic foundation of admissible non-injective
    transitions~\cite{FoundationM}, we derive the structure of gauge theory without
    introducing it as a postulate.
    Gauge charges are identified as invariants of equivalence classes in the projection
    fibre: two configurations are gauge-equivalent when they project to the same
    observable state.
    The group of fibre-preserving transformations constitutes the gauge group.
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is identified as the unique minimal non-abelian admissible group
    from the quaternionic structure of the admissible fibre; $\mathrm{U}(1)$ arises as
    the residual phase symmetry; $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is a structurally motivated candidate
    extension whose derivation remains open.
    The gauge connection arises as the infinitesimal generator of fibre transport under
    varying projection, and Yang\textendash Mills dynamics emerge from minimising the
    resulting curvature.
    No dynamical substrate is required at any step; the derivations rest solely on
    Axioms~A1--A4 of~\cite{FoundationM} and the spectral results of the O-series.
    Effective spacetime geometry, requiring the continuum limit of~\cite{Q5a},
    is left for a companion paper.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    gauge symmetry; non-injective projection; fibre bundles; gauge invariance; Yang–Mills theory; emergent gauge
    structure; admissibility; Heisenberg group; SU(2); quaternionic structure

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  \subsection*{Position in the programme}

    The companion papers~\cite{FoundationM,Q1,Q2} establish the quantum-mechanical
    structure of the Cosmochrony framework: the proto-state, phase coherence, the
    Born rule, the singlet correlator, and the Tsirelson bound, all derived from the
    admissibility structure of non-injective projection.
    These results concern the \emph{internal} structure of the admissible fibre at a
    single transition step.

    The present paper addresses one macroscopic consequence of allowing the projection
    structure to vary from point to point in the effective continuum: the conserved
    invariants of the projection fibre and the symmetry group they generate.
    The answer is gauge charges and gauge groups (§\ref{sec:charges}), with
    Yang\textendash Mills dynamics emerging from fibre transport (§\ref{sec:ym}).
    Effective spacetime geometry, which depends on the continuum limit
    of~\cite{Q5a}, is left for a companion paper.

  \subsection*{What is and is not derived in this paper}

    The present work establishes the structural origin of gauge symmetry and gauge
    connection from admissible non-injective projection.
    It is not intended as a full reconstruction of the Standard Model, but as an
    identification of the minimal structures that must emerge at the effective level.

    This paper derives the \emph{structure} of gauge theory from the admissibility
    framework.
    It does not derive specific coupling constants or the precise matter content of
    the Standard Model.
    These remain open and are identified as directions for future work in
    Section~\ref{sec:open}.

    The derivations are structural: they identify which gauge groups are
    \emph{admissible} given the constraints, not which specific values their
    parameters take.

  \subsection*{Organisation}

    Section~\ref{sec:charges} derives gauge charges as fibre invariants,
    identifies the gauge group, and establishes $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ as the unique
    minimal non-abelian admissible group.
    Section~\ref{sec:connection} derives the gauge connection as the infinitesimal
    generator of fibre transport.
    Section~\ref{sec:ym} derives Yang\textendash Mills dynamics from the curvature of
    this connection.
    Section~\ref{sec:open} identifies the open directions.

  \section{Gauge Charges as Fibre Invariants}
  \label{sec:charges}

  \subsection{Charges from non-injectivity}

    The non-injectivity of $\Pi_n$ (Axiom~A2 of~\cite{FoundationM}) means that
    multiple configurations in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ project to the same observable
    state $O_n$.
    These configurations are physically indistinguishable at the observable level
    but may carry distinct internal structure.

    \begin{definition}[Gauge charge]
      \label{def:gauge-charge}
      A \emph{gauge charge} is a functional
      \begin{equation}
        Q : \Omega_n^{(c)} \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{R}
      \end{equation}
      that is constant on projection fibres:
      \begin{equation}
        \Pi_n(\omega_1) = \Pi_n(\omega_2)
        \;\Longrightarrow\;
        Q(\omega_1) = Q(\omega_2).
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    A gauge charge is therefore an observable that cannot be resolved by $\Pi_n$
    itself --- it lives in the kernel structure of $\Pi_n$ --- but is well-defined
    on the fibre.

    \begin{definition}[Gauge group]
      \label{def:gauge-group}
      The \emph{gauge group} $\mathcal{G}$ is the group of transformations
      $\omega \mapsto g \cdot \omega$ that preserve the projection:
      \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{G}
        \;:=\;
        \bigl\{g \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Omega_n^{(c)}) \mid
        \Pi_n(g \cdot \omega) = \Pi_n(\omega) \text{ for all } \omega \bigr\}.
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    $\mathcal{G}$ consists precisely of the fibre-preserving symmetries: it acts
    within $\Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ without affecting the observable output.

    \begin{remark}[Gauge invariance as structural necessity]
        The gauge group is not introduced by hand.
        It is forced by the structure of $\Pi_n$: as soon as $\Pi_n$ is non-injective,
        there exist non-trivial fibre-preserving transformations, and hence a
        non-trivial gauge group.
        Gauge invariance is a structural consequence of Axiom~A2, not an additional
        postulate.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Structural constraints on admissible gauge groups}
    \label{subsec:gauge-constraints}

    Not every group is admissible under Definition~\ref{def:gauge-group}.
    Three structural constraints restrict the possible gauge groups.

    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item \emph{Non-factorisability.}
        By Axiom~A3, the admissible fibre cannot decompose into independent components
        at each transition.
        This excludes purely abelian direct-product structures beyond a single residual
        phase factor.

        \item \emph{Compatibility with the admissible algebra.}
        The admissible fibre is identified with the Weil representation of
        $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$~\cite{FoundationM},
        whose continuous limit admits a quaternionic minimal structure~\cite{Q2,O23}.
        By the classification of normed division algebras, the non-abelian part of
        $\mathcal{G}$ must embed into the group of unit quaternions, which is
        $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.

        \item \emph{Stability under admissibility refinement.}
        Under spectral refinement (large-$q$ limit), admissible sectors must remain
        closed under the projection-induced transport.
        This favours compact Lie groups with stable representation theory.
    \end{enumerate}

    These constraints do not uniquely determine the full gauge group, but they
    identify $\mathrm{U}(1)$ as the residual phase symmetry (constraint (i)),
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ as the minimal non-abelian admissible sector (constraint (ii)),
    and suggest $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a higher-dimensional extension compatible with
    fibre-preserving actions (constraint (iii), open).

  \subsection{Identification of the gauge groups}

    The three candidates are identified as follows.

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{$\mathrm{U}(1)$}: global phase rotations
      $\omega \mapsto e^{i\theta}\omega$ within the complex fibre $\mathbb{C}^q$.
      These preserve $|\omega|$ and hence the observable norm, while acting
      non-trivially on the phase --- which is in the kernel of $\Pi_n$ by the phase
      invariance of $\sigma_c(n)$~\cite{O12}.

      \item \emph{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$}: the stable fixed point of the admissibility
      flow established in~\cite{Q2} Theorem~8.1.
      Its fibre-preserving action on $\mathbb{C}^2$ is the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
      structure derived in~\cite{Q1}.
      Its identification as the \emph{unique} minimal non-abelian admissible group is
      established as Theorem~\ref{thm:su2-rigidity} below.

      \item \emph{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$}: a structurally motivated candidate for a
      higher-dimensional internal symmetry of the fibre, corresponding to multiple
      co-admissible internal directions beyond $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
      Its identification from the O-series admissibility data is an open direction
      (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
    \end{itemize}

    \begin{remark}[Status of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$]
        The identification of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a gauge group is a structural
        hypothesis, not a derived result.
        Establishing it would require finding, within the O-series spectral data, a
        co-admissibility pattern for a group with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ structure analogous
        to the $\lambda_{1/2} = \lambda_{3/2}$ co-admissibility of~\cite{Q2}.
        This is identified as a medium-term open direction.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Rigidity of the minimal non-abelian sector}
    \label{subsec:su2-rigidity}

    \begin{theorem}[Minimal admissible gauge sector]
      \label{thm:su2-rigidity}
      Under Axioms~A1--A3 and Born\textendash Infeld parity, the unique minimal
      non-abelian compact Lie group compatible with admissible non-injective
      projection is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}[Structural argument]
        Axiom~A3 (Born\textendash Infeld admissibility) forbids factorisation of the
        fibre, enforcing a non-trivial commutator structure on the admissible algebra.
        By Foundation Theorem~5.6~\cite{FoundationM}, the admissible fibre $F_n$ is
        identified with the Weil representation $V_\rho$ of
        $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
        In the continuous admissible limit, the Born\textendash Infeld parity
        involution and the quaternionic minimality result of O23~\cite{O23} force the
        image of any admissible morphism into $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$, the imaginary
        part of the quaternion algebra.
        This three-dimensional space is isomorphic to the Lie algebra
        $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
        By the quaternionic rigidity established in O27~\cite{O27}, any admissible
        morphism satisfying the three structural constraints of that paper necessarily
        factors through $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
        Exponentiation yields $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ as the unique minimal non-abelian
        compact group.
        Among compact Lie groups, minimality forces a simple rank-one group; the only
        such group compatible with the quaternionic constraint is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}
      Theorem~\ref{thm:su2-rigidity} is not a phenomenological selection:
      $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the \emph{forced} output of the admissibility constraints,
      not a candidate chosen to match observations.
      The full details of the quaternionic rigidity argument are established
      in~\cite{O23,O27}.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The Gauge Connection}
  \label{sec:connection}

  \subsection{The problem of comparing neighbouring fibres}

    The following development is conditional on two structural hypotheses.
    First, the admissible fibre $F_n$ admits an effective continuum description
    in which it forms a smooth fibre bundle over an effective spacetime manifold.
    Second, this manifold itself carries a differentiable structure, which requires
    the emergence of a continuum geometry from the discrete admissibility filter ---
    a step established only conditionally in~\cite{Q5a} and not proved here.
    Neither hypothesis is an axiom of the present framework; both are working
    assumptions whose justification is deferred to~\cite{Q5a} and a companion paper
    on effective geometry.
    All results in §\ref{sec:connection}--\ref{sec:ym} are therefore conditional
    on these two hypotheses.

    For each point $x$ in the effective manifold, the fibre $F_x = \Pi^{-1}(x)$
    carries the internal gauge structure of Section~\ref{sec:charges}.

    When the projection $\Pi(x)$ varies from point to point, the fibres $F_x$ and
    $F_{x+dx}$ are not canonically identified.
    A state $\psi(x)$ transforming under $\mathcal{G}$ acquires an additional term
    under differentiation:
    \begin{equation}
      \partial_\mu(g(x)\,\psi(x))
      \;=\; g(x)\,\partial_\mu\psi(x) + (\partial_\mu g(x))\,\psi(x),
    \end{equation}
    which is not covariant under local gauge transformations $g(x) \in \mathcal{G}$.

  \subsection{Connection as fibre transport operator}
    \label{subsec:transport}

    A comparison between fibres at neighbouring points $x$ and $x + dx$
    requires the specification of a transport map
    \begin{equation}
      T_{x \to x+dx} : F_x \;\longrightarrow\; F_{x+dx}.
    \end{equation}
    Because the projection $\Pi$ is non-injective, there is no canonical
    identification between fibres at distinct points.
    The transport map is therefore defined only up to a fibre-preserving
    transformation, i.e.\ an element of the gauge group $\mathcal{G}$.

    The gauge connection arises as the infinitesimal generator of this transport:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:transport-expansion}
      T_{x \to x+dx}
      \;=\; \mathrm{Id} + A_\mu(x)\,dx^\mu + o(dx),
    \end{equation}
    where $A_\mu(x)$ takes values in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of the gauge
    group.
    This definition does not rely on a background bundle structure: the connection
    is the minimal structure required to compare admissible fibres under variation
    of the effective state.

  \subsection{Gauge connection from fibre transport}

    \begin{definition}[Gauge connection]
      \label{def:connection}
      A \emph{gauge connection} $A_\mu$ is the $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form
      appearing in~\eqref{eq:transport-expansion}, defining a covariant derivative
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:covariant-derivative}
        D_\mu \;:=\; \partial_\mu + A_\mu
      \end{equation}
      such that $D_\mu\psi$ transforms homogeneously under local gauge
      transformations.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{proposition}[Admissibility interpretation of $A_\mu$]
      \label{prop:connection-admissibility}
      In the projection framework, $A_\mu(x)$ encodes the local variation of the
      admissible fibre structure: it is the infinitesimal correction needed to
      transport an admissible configuration from the fibre at $x$ to the fibre at
      $x + dx$ consistently with the admissibility constraints.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}[Structural argument]
        The admissibility constraints (A1--A3 of~\cite{FoundationM}) define, at each
        point $x$, a fibre $F_x$ of structurally compatible configurations.
        When $\Pi(x)$ varies, the fibres $F_x$ and $F_{x+dx}$ are related by the
        differential of the projection map.
        Consistency of the admissibility condition across neighbouring fibres ---
        i.e.\ that a configuration admissible at $x$ maps to an admissible
        configuration at $x + dx$ --- requires a linear correction of the form
        $A_\mu(x)\,dx^\mu$ taking values in $\mathfrak{g}$.
        This correction is precisely the gauge connection of
        Definition~\ref{def:connection}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Relation to the holonomy argument]
        The white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} establishes that non-injective admissible
        projections generically have non-trivial holonomy, which is the minimal
        structural condition for a gauge symmetry to arise as a descriptive invariance.
        Proposition~\ref{prop:connection-admissibility} is the local infinitesimal
        version of this global statement.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Yang\textendash Mills Dynamics}
  \label{sec:ym}

  \subsection{Curvature as obstruction to fibre consistency}

    Given the transport map of §\ref{subsec:transport}, consider two infinitesimal
    displacements $dx^\mu$ and $dx^\nu$ from a point $x$.
    The failure of successive transports along these directions to commute defines
    the curvature of the connection.

    \begin{definition}[Field strength]
      \label{def:field-strength}
      The \emph{field strength} associated with the gauge connection $A_\mu$ is the
      curvature of the covariant derivative:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:field-strength}
        F_{\mu\nu} \;:=\; [D_\mu, D_\nu]
        \;=\; \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu + [A_\mu, A_\nu].
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    $F_{\mu\nu}$ measures the obstruction to consistent identification of admissible
    fibres over infinitesimal loops --- equivalently, the non-commutativity of the
    admissibility constraints in two neighbouring directions.
    A flat connection ($F_{\mu\nu} = 0$) corresponds to a globally consistent fibre
    structure; non-zero curvature signals inconsistency in the transport of admissible
    configurations.

  \subsection{Yang\textendash Mills action from admissibility minimisation}

    \paragraph{Structural origin of Yang\textendash Mills-type dynamics.}
      The admissibility principle (A3 of~\cite{FoundationM}) favours configurations
      that preserve the full multiplicity of open directions without premature
      selection.
      Translated to the gauge connection, this principle selects configurations that
      minimise the obstruction to consistent fibre transport, i.e.\ minimise the
      curvature $\|F_{\mu\nu}\|^2$.

      The Cosmochrony-specific contribution is the identification of $F_{\mu\nu}$ as
      the natural curvature object: it follows from the non-injectivity of $\Pi$
      (via fibre transport, §\ref{subsec:transport}) without postulating a gauge field.
      The subsequent step --- that the unique second-order gauge-invariant
      Lorentz-invariant functional of $F_{\mu\nu}$ is $\int\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\mu\nu}
  F^{\mu\nu})$ --- is a standard result of gauge theory, not specific to the
      present framework.

      \begin{remark}[Yang\textendash Mills action]
        \label{rem:ym-action}
        Under the continuum hypotheses of §\ref{sec:connection}, the minimisation
        principle above selects the action
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:ym-action}
          S_{\mathrm{YM}}[A]
          \;=\; \int \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}\bigr)\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x,
        \end{equation}
        whose variation yields the Yang\textendash Mills equations $D_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$.
        This is a structural motivation, not a derivation from Axioms~A1--A4 alone.
        The uniqueness of~\eqref{eq:ym-action} follows from standard EFT power-counting
        (second order in $A_\mu$, gauge-invariant, Lorentz-invariant); it does not
        require the Cosmochrony framework.
        What the framework contributes is the non-postulated origin of $F_{\mu\nu}$
        itself.
        A direct derivation of~\eqref{eq:ym-action} from the axioms via the
        Born\textendash Infeld limit is discussed in~\cite{BornInfeld}; derivation from
        Axioms~A1--A4 alone remains open (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
      \end{remark}

  \section{Open Directions}
  \label{sec:open}

  \paragraph{Direct derivation of Yang\textendash Mills uniqueness.}
    Remark~\ref{rem:ym-action} gives a structural motivation for the
    Yang\textendash Mills action.
    Its uniqueness --- that $S_{\mathrm{YM}}$ is the \emph{only}
    admissibility-compatible second-order action --- follows from the
    Born\textendash Infeld limit~\cite{BornInfeld} but not yet from Foundation~M
    axioms alone.

  \paragraph{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ from admissibility.}
    The identification of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a gauge group requires finding a
    co-admissibility pattern for an $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-type group in the O-series
    spectral data.
    The analogue of the $\lambda_{1/2} = \lambda_{3/2}$ result on $2I$ (established
    in~\cite{Q2}) must be found for a group with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ structure.

  \paragraph{Coupling constants.}
    The present paper identifies which gauge groups are admissible.
    It does not determine the numerical values of coupling constants
    ($g_s$, $g_w$, $\alpha$).
    These are expected to emerge from the quantitative spectral data of the O-series
    via the transfer chain $c_\chi \to \delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \to \beta^* \approx 0.126$,
    but the explicit identification is open.
